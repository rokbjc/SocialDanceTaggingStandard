## Specific instruments

<a id="piano"></a>

### 🎹 Piano `p`

tier: specific

Piano carries the melody and harmonic drive of the track, from stride and boogie left-hand pumps to single-note bop lines.

**Distinguishing trait:** a polyphonic keyboard lead that holds rhythm and chords at once, unlike the single-line reed or brass leads. Count Basie and Fats Waller are the swing-era touchstones.

<sub>Aliases: Piano Led</sub>

<a id="clarinet"></a>

### 🎶 Clarinet `cl`

tier: specific

Clarinet leads with a fluid, woody upper-register voice that soars over the band, the signature solo instrument of the swing era.

**Distinguishing trait:** brighter and more agile than the saxophone, its reed sibling, with a liquid legato Goodman and Shaw made famous on uptempo swing.

<sub>Aliases: Clarinet Led</sub>

<a id="saxophone"></a>

### 🎷 Saxophone `sax`

tier: specific

Saxophone leads with a warm, breathy reed tone, from Hawkins' big tenor to Parker's fast alto lines.

**Distinguishing trait:** rounder and heavier than the clarinet, its reed sibling, and the backbone of any sax-section feature. Lester Young and Stan Getz mark the lighter, cooler end.

<sub>Aliases: Saxophone Led · Sax Led</sub>

<a id="trumpet"></a>

### 🎺 Trumpet `tp`

tier: specific

Trumpet (or cornet) leads with a bright, piercing brass voice that cuts through the band, equally at home on hot-jazz breaks and bop.

**Distinguishing trait:** higher and more brilliant than the trombone, its brass sibling. Louis Armstrong is the foundational example; Dizzy Gillespie and Miles Davis carry it into bop and beyond.

<sub>Aliases: Trumpet Led · Cornet · Cornet Led</sub>

<a id="trombone"></a>

### 📯 Trombone `tb`

tier: specific

Trombone leads with a smooth, sliding brass voice in the lower-middle register, capable of growls, glissandos, and singing ballads.

**Distinguishing trait:** warmer and deeper than the trumpet, its brass sibling. Jack Teagarden and Tommy Dorsey define the swing-era lead.

<sub>Aliases: Trombone Led</sub>

<a id="guitar"></a>

### 🎸 Guitar `g`

tier: specific

Guitar takes the lead line, from acoustic Gypsy-swing runs to amplified single-string solos.

**Distinguishing trait:** a plucked string lead with sharper attack than the bowed violin, its string sibling. Charlie Christian pioneered the electric jazz solo; Django Reinhardt defines the acoustic Manouche lead.

<sub>Aliases: Guitar Led</sub>

<a id="violin"></a>

### 🎻 Violin `vln`

tier: specific

Violin (or fiddle) leads with a singing, bowed string voice, swinging in Gypsy jazz and sawing in Western Swing.

**Distinguishing trait:** a sustained, bowed lead with continuous tone, unlike the plucked attack of the guitar or banjo. Stéphane Grappelli is the jazz touchstone; Bob Wills the Western-Swing one.

<sub>Aliases: Violin Led · Fiddle · Fiddle Led</sub>

<a id="banjo"></a>

### 🪕 Banjo `bjo`

tier: specific

Banjo leads with a bright, percussive, fast-decaying string tone central to early hot jazz and trad bands.

**Distinguishing trait:** sharper and more clipped than the sustaining guitar, its string sibling, giving an older-sounding chank. Johnny St. Cyr of the Hot Five and Eddie Condon are the classic examples.

<sub>Aliases: Banjo Led</sub>

<a id="harmonica"></a>

### 🎼 Harmonica `hrm`

tier: specific

Harmonica (harp) leads with a reedy, vocal, bent-note voice, most at home in blues.

**Distinguishing trait:** a free-reed lead with expressive pitch-bending unlike any of the brass or string instruments. Little Walter and Sonny Boy Williamson are the amplified-blues benchmarks.

<sub>Aliases: Harmonica Led · Harp · Harp Led</sub>

<a id="organ"></a>

### 🎵 Organ `org`

tier: specific

Organ leads with the sustained, drawbar-rich voice of the Hammond B-3, often grooving over its own bass pedals in soul-jazz and jump blues.

**Distinguishing trait:** a held, swelling keyboard tone with no decay, unlike the percussive attack of the piano. Jimmy Smith and Jack McDuff are the touchstones.

<sub>Aliases: Organ Led · Hammond · Hammond Led · Hammond B-3</sub>

<a id="vibraphone"></a>

### 🔔 Vibraphone `vib`

tier: specific

Vibraphone (vibes) leads with a shimmering, metallic, mallet-struck tone, often with a slow tremolo.

**Distinguishing trait:** a tuned-percussion lead with a bell-like sustain unlike the brass, reed, or string voices. Lionel Hampton brought it into swing; Milt Jackson into bop.

<sub>Aliases: Vibraphone Led · Vibes · Vibes Led · Vibes-led</sub>

<a id="accordion"></a>

### 🪗 Accordion `acc`

tier: specific

Accordion leads with a reedy, bellows-driven voice that can sustain and swell, prominent in zydeco, musette, and tango.

**Distinguishing trait:** a free-reed lead capable of chords and held tones unlike the single-line harmonica. Clifton Chenier (zydeco) and Astor Piazzolla (tango) mark the range.

<sub>Aliases: Accordion Led</sub>

<a id="drums"></a>

### 🥁 Drums `dr`

tier: specific

Drums take the lead through extended kit features, solos, and tom-tom hooks rather than just keeping time.

**Distinguishing trait:** an unpitched percussion lead, the rhythmic counterpart to the pitched bass within the rhythm family. Gene Krupa and Buddy Rich are the swing-era showcases.

<sub>Aliases: Drums Led</sub>

<a id="bass"></a>

### 🎸 Bass `b`

tier: specific

Bass takes the lead through walking features, solos, and prominent bass lines, on double bass or bass guitar.

**Distinguishing trait:** the pitched low-end lead of the rhythm family, paired with the unpitched drums. Jimmy Blanton pioneered the solo role; Paul Chambers and Mingus carried it into bop.

<sub>Aliases: Bass Led · Bass Guitar · Double Bass</sub>

<a id="tuba"></a>

### 🎺 Tuba `tba`

tier: specific

Tuba (or sousaphone) supplies the walking bass line in a brass band or early-jazz rhythm section.

**Distinguishing trait:** the lowest brass voice, a section/credit instrument that rarely takes the lead.

<sub>Aliases: Sousaphone · Bass Horn</sub>

<a id="cello"></a>

### 🎻 Cello `vc`

tier: specific

Cello adds a warm low-string voice, occasionally bowed or plucked as a solo colour but usually a section/credit instrument.

**Distinguishing trait:** lower and darker than the violin, its string sibling.

<sub>Aliases: Violoncello</sub>

<a id="flute"></a>

### 🎶 Flute `fl`

tier: specific

Flute contributes a light, airy woodwind colour, most often a section or credit voice in swing and blues rather than the lead.

**Distinguishing trait:** a breathy, high woodwind unlike the reed or brass leads.

<sub>Aliases: Flutes</sub>

<a id="washboard"></a>

### 🥁 Washboard `wb`

tier: specific

Washboard provides scraped hand-percussion in jug bands and early hot jazz.

**Distinguishing trait:** an unpitched credit/rhythm instrument, the folk cousin of the drum kit.

<sub>Aliases: Frottoir</sub>

<a id="percussion"></a>

### 🥁 Percussion `perc`

tier: specific

Percussion covers hand and auxiliary percussion (congas, bongos, timbales, shakers) beyond the drum kit.

**Distinguishing trait:** an unpitched credit/rhythm voice added for texture, not a lead.

<sub>Aliases: Percussions</sub>

## Generic families

<a id="reed"></a>

### 🎷 Reed `Rd`

tier: generic

Generic family covering clarinet- and saxophone-led tracks, including reed-section features.

**Distinguishing trait:** use when the lead is clearly a reed voice or a sax/clarinet section, but the specific instrument is unknown or shifts across the track. A Miller-style sax chase or big-band sax section is the typical case.

<sub>Aliases: Reed Led · Reeds · Reeds Led</sub>

<a id="brass"></a>

### 🎺 Brass `Br`

tier: generic

Generic family covering trumpet- and trombone-led tracks, including brass-section shouts and features.

**Distinguishing trait:** use when the lead is clearly brass or a horn section, but the specific instrument is unknown or alternates. Ellington brass features and big-band brass shouts are the typical case.

<sub>Aliases: Brass Led · Horns · Horns Led</sub>

<a id="string"></a>

### 🎻 String `Str`

tier: generic

Generic family covering guitar-, violin-, and banjo-led tracks.

**Distinguishing trait:** use when the lead is a plucked or bowed string ensemble, or alternates among strings, rather than one identifiable instrument. A Manouche ensemble (Django plus Grappelli) or a Western-Swing fiddle-and-steel front line is the typical case.

<sub>Aliases: String Led · Strings · Strings Led</sub>

<a id="vocal"></a>

### 🎤 Vocal `Voc`

tier: generic

Generic family for vocal-led tracks where the voice is the lead instrument.

**Distinguishing trait:** covers no specific sub-instruments; use whenever a singer fronts the track, across jazz, blues, and gospel. Ella, Sinatra, Bessie Smith, and Mahalia Jackson span the range.

<sub>Aliases: Vocal Led · Vocals Led · Voice Led</sub>

<a id="rhythm"></a>

### 🥁 Rhythm `Rhy`

tier: generic

Generic family covering drums- and bass-led tracks where the rhythm section is the feature.

**Distinguishing trait:** use when the lead is the rhythm section as a whole, or shifts between drums and bass, rather than a melodic instrument. Gene Krupa drum features and walking-bass focus are the typical cases.

<sub>Aliases: Rhythm Led · Rhythm Section</sub>

<a id="novelty"></a>

### ✨ Novelty `Nov`

tier: generic

Generic catch-all family for less-common lead instruments: harmonica, organ, vibraphone, accordion, and banjo.

**Distinguishing trait:** use when the lead falls outside the main reed, brass, string, and rhythm families. Clifton Chenier on accordion or a vibes feature is the typical case.

<sub>Aliases: Novelty Led</sub>
