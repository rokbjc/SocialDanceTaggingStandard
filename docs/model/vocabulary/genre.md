## Swing

<a id="genre-big-band"></a>

<a id="big-band"></a>

### 🎻 Big Band `BB`

family: [swing](#fam-swing) · era: 1930–1945 · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 100–280

The large ensemble format (12-25 players) organized into sax, trumpet, trombone and rhythm sections that defined the Swing Era.

**Distinguishing trait:** a 'wall of sound' with sectional riffs where the shout chorus and ride-out are the most explicit and dynamically distinct of any swing genre; it's a format, not a genre.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Walking 4](rhythmic_feel.md#walking-4)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [AABA](song_form.md#aaba) · [12-bar Blues](song_form.md#12-bar-blues) · [Rhythm Changes](song_form.md#rhythm-changes)

<sub>Aliases: Big Band Jazz · BigBand · Large Ensemble Swing</sub>

### 🇬🇧 British Dance Band `BDB`

family: [swing](#fam-swing) · era: 1925–1945 · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 110–200

British dance orchestras of the 1920s-40s — the UK equivalent of American swing big bands but more polished and restrained, distributed via BBC radio and London hotels (Ambrose, Ray Noble).

**Distinguishing trait:** more 'sweet', melodic and elegant with less improvisation than American swing, centered on a single vocal refrain chorus.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Two-Beat](rhythmic_feel.md#two-beat) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [AABA](song_form.md#aaba) · [ABAC](song_form.md#abac) · [AABA with Verse](song_form.md#aaba-with-verse)

### 🪕 Gypsy Jazz `GYP`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 1934– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 100–280

A uniquely European acoustic style created by Django Reinhardt in 1930s France, fusing Roma (manouche) tradition with American jazz.

**Distinguishing trait:** the dry, percussive 'la pompe' rhythm guitar and a string-only lineup (no drums, no horns), with virtuosic lead guitar and violin.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Manouche Swing](rhythmic_feel.md#manouche-swing) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths)

<a id="hot-jazz"></a>

### 🔥 Hot Jazz `HOT`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 1923–1935 · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 120–250

The 1920s evolution of New Orleans Jazz led by Louis Armstrong's Hot Five/Seven, marking the historic shift from collective improvisation to individual soloing.

**Distinguishing trait:** a featured soloist (Armstrong's trumpet) carries a whole chorus, with stop-time breaks and scat singing, before the collective ride-out.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Two-Beat](rhythmic_feel.md#two-beat) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths)

### 🎷 Jazz `JZ`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 1910– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high)

Improvised African-American music born in New Orleans in the early 20th century and the umbrella for swing, bebop and beyond, defined by swing feel and improvisation. Distinguishing trait (as the catalog's general label): the broad jazz tradition encompassing all its sub-styles.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Walking 4](rhythmic_feel.md#walking-4)

<a id="jazz-blues"></a>

### 🎺 Jazz Blues `JzB`

family: [blues](#fam-blues) · era: 1925– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 60–200

A crossover classification for tunes that are simultaneously jazz and blues — the 12-bar form played with jazz harmony, improvisation, and swing feel.

**Distinguishing trait:** it works on both the swing and blues dance floors, sitting in the space between pure jazz (too much blues form/feel) and pure blues (too much jazz harmony).

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Walking 4](rhythmic_feel.md#walking-4) · [Bebop](rhythmic_feel.md#bebop)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [12-bar Blues](song_form.md#12-bar-blues) · [Rhythm Changes](song_form.md#rhythm-changes) · [AABA](song_form.md#aaba)

<a id="jump-blues"></a>

### ⚡ Jump Blues `JB`

family: [blues](#fam-blues) · era: 1940–1955 · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 130–200

An energetic, rowdy late-1940s combo style — small band, strong backbeat, honking saxes, blues shouting, often humorous lyrics; the bridge between Swing and rock'n'roll.

**Distinguishing trait:** a big-band arc compressed into a combo, with a honking sax solo and riff-driven shout chorus as the climax over the 12-bar form.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jump Shuffle](rhythmic_feel.md#jump-shuffle) · [Shuffle](rhythmic_feel.md#shuffle) · [Boogie Shuffle](rhythmic_feel.md#boogie-shuffle)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [12-bar Blues](song_form.md#12-bar-blues) · [AABA](song_form.md#aaba) · [8-bar Blues](song_form.md#8-bar-blues)

### 🌾 Kansas City Jazz `KCJ`

family: [swing](#fam-swing) · era: 1928–1945 · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 120–220

A regional 1930s jazz style from Kansas City's 24-hour jam-session culture, more blues-based and relaxed than New York swing.

**Distinguishing trait:** riff-based 'head arrangements' (by ear, not written) with a loose, laid-back groove, dominant saxophones, and antiphonal shout riffs (vs. the composed shout chorus of NY swing).

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Walking 4](rhythmic_feel.md#walking-4)

### 💃 Modern Swing Revival `MSR`

family: [swing](#fam-swing) · era: 2005– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 100–220

Contemporary (2000+) bands playing vintage-accurate swing built for Lindy Hop dancers, not rock festivals — the heart of today's scene.

**Distinguishing trait:** small combos with traditional arrangements and a deliberate vintage/analog recording sound (it sounds like 1938 recorded in 2018), vs. the rock-infused Neo-Swing.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Walking 4](rhythmic_feel.md#walking-4) · [Jump Shuffle](rhythmic_feel.md#jump-shuffle)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [AABA](song_form.md#aaba) · [12-bar Blues](song_form.md#12-bar-blues) · [Rhythm Changes](song_form.md#rhythm-changes)

### ♻️ NOLA Jazz Revival `NJR`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 2000– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 80–250

Contemporary (2000+) bands, mostly American and often from the New Orleans Frenchmen Street scene, playing traditional collective-improvisation NOLA jazz.

**Distinguishing trait:** defined by being modern bands playing the traditional two-beat NOLA style (vs. 1920s-40s originals and the 1950s British Trad revival).

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Two-Beat](rhythmic_feel.md#two-beat) · [Second Line](rhythmic_feel.md#second-line) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths)

### ⚜️ New Orleans Jazz `NOJ`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 1910–1935 · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 100–220

The birthplace of jazz — collective improvisation of cornet, clarinet and trombone over a rhythm section with a marching 2-beat 'second line' feel.

**Distinguishing trait:** three-voice polyphony where the whole front line improvises at once toward an ensemble ride-out, rather than individual solos.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Two-Beat](rhythmic_feel.md#two-beat) · [Second Line](rhythmic_feel.md#second-line) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths)

### 🎶 Swing `SW`

family: [swing](#fam-swing) · era: 1930–1945 · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 120–250

The golden age of jazz and dance music (~1935-1946), defined by the 4-beat swing feel with its 'lilting' long-short eighth notes.

**Distinguishing trait:** arrangement-driven big-band structure over a walking bass, building through call-and-response between sections to a shout chorus and ride-out climax.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Walking 4](rhythmic_feel.md#walking-4)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [AABA](song_form.md#aaba) · [12-bar Blues](song_form.md#12-bar-blues) · [Rhythm Changes](song_form.md#rhythm-changes)

### 🎺 Trad Jazz `TRD`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 1940– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 100–200

The British revival of New Orleans and Dixieland jazz that peaked in the late 1950s (Chris Barber, Acker Bilk, Kenny Ball).

**Distinguishing trait:** a 'cleaned-up', smoother, more dance-friendly and arranged version of early collective-improvisation jazz, less raw than the 1920s originals.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Two-Beat](rhythmic_feel.md#two-beat) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths)

### 🎙️ Vocal Jazz `VOC`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 1930– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 60–200

Vocal interpretation of the Great American Songbook — the voice as an instrument where phrasing, timing and personal expression matter more than raw power (Ella, Sinatra, Billie Holiday).

**Distinguishing trait:** a sung 32-bar AABA chorus, with improvisation pared to a half-chorus scat or instrumental solo, and the narrative verse often dropped.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Walking 4](rhythmic_feel.md#walking-4)

### 📖 Adult Standards `STD`

family: [pop](#fam-pop) · era: 1940– · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 60–180

The Great American Songbook — the canon of classic American popular songs from the 1920s-60s that became enduring jazz standards (Porter, Gershwin, Berlin).

**Distinguishing trait:** as a tag it marks these songs regardless of performer or arrangement, typically in 32-bar AABA form with sophisticated lyrics and jazz harmony.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths)

### 🎺 Dixieland `DIX`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 1917– · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 120–240

The Chicago evolution of New Orleans Jazz, often played by white musicians, with faster tempos and a more polished, 'tight' sound (the ODJB cut the first jazz record, 1917).

**Distinguishing trait:** more organized than New Orleans Jazz, with clearer separate solos, trading, frequent breaks, less blues feeling and more 'entertainment' energy.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Two-Beat](rhythmic_feel.md#two-beat) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths)

family: [rock](#fam-rock) · era: 1954–1965 · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 110–180

The 1950s fusion of R&B, boogie woogie, country and gospel (Chuck Berry, Little Richard, Jerry Lee Lewis) — relevant to swing in its pre-1960 form.

**Distinguishing trait:** boogie-woogie piano patterns transferred to guitar over a strong backbeat and 12-bar blues form, still retaining the shuffle feel it came from.

### 🎛️ Electro Swing `ESW`

family: [electro](#fam-electro) · era: 2005– · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 110–140

Electronic music that samples or imitates 1920s-40s swing/jazz over a modern house/electro beat (Parov Stelar, Caravan Palace).

**Distinguishing trait:** it is electronic production sampling swing built around a build-and-drop loop (vs. Neo-Swing, which is real instruments actually playing swing); controversial in the swing scene.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Straight 4](rhythmic_feel.md#straight-4) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths)

### 🕺 Neo-Swing `NSW`

family: [swing](#fam-swing) · era: 1990–2005 · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 130–220

A 1990s-2000s retro swing revival started by ex-punk musicians in LA (Royal Crown Revue, Big Bad Voodoo Daddy), peaking in 1998.

**Distinguishing trait:** vintage swing form charged with punk/rock energy and electric guitar (vs. the vintage-accurate Modern Swing Revival, which aims to sound like a 1938 recording).

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jump Shuffle](rhythmic_feel.md#jump-shuffle) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Backbeat](rhythmic_feel.md#backbeat)

### 🎹 Ragtime `RAG`

family: [ragtime](#fam-ragtime) · era: 1895–1920 · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 80–140

Composed piano music with a syncopated right-hand melody over a steady march-like 'oom-pah' bass — the pre-jazz parlour style of Scott Joplin.

**Distinguishing trait:** everything is written down with no improvisation, in a multi-strain AABBACCDD form (vs. the improvised swing of stride and jazz).

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Two-Beat](rhythmic_feel.md#two-beat) · [Stride](rhythmic_feel.md#stride)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Ragtime](song_form.md#ragtime) · [AABB](song_form.md#aabb)

### 🎸 Rockabilly `RKB`

family: [rock](#fam-rock) · era: 1954–1958 · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 130–200

A mid-1950s Memphis fusion of country and R&B, mostly young white Southerners (Elvis at Sun Records); the name combines 'rock' + 'hillbilly'.

**Distinguishing trait:** driving uptempo energy with percussive slap bass, slapback echo and 'hiccup' vocals in a minimal trio lineup (the raw core of early rock'n'roll).

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Rockabilly](rhythmic_feel.md#rockabilly) · [Backbeat](rhythmic_feel.md#backbeat) · [Boogie Shuffle](rhythmic_feel.md#boogie-shuffle)

### 🎹 Stride Piano `STR`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 1920–1940 · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 80–200

A Harlem evolution of ragtime where the left hand 'strides' between bass note and chord while the right hand improvises virtuosic melodies.

**Distinguishing trait:** it added improvisation, faster tempos, and swing feel to ragtime's structure — these were among the first true jazz improvisers (vs. fully-composed ragtime).

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Stride](rhythmic_feel.md#stride) · [Two-Beat](rhythmic_feel.md#two-beat) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths)

### 🍬 Sweet Band `SwB`

family: [swing](#fam-swing) · era: 1928–1948 · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 80–180

A romantic, smooth branch of 1920s-40s dance-band music (Guy Lombardo) set against the 'hot' swing of Goodman/Basie.

**Distinguishing trait:** legato, polished, dance-friendly playing with little improvisation, more violins and crooner vocals — built for hotel ballrooms (vs. the punchy, solo-driven hot swing).

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Two-Beat](rhythmic_feel.md#two-beat) · [Waltz](rhythmic_feel.md#waltz)

### 🇩🇪 Tanzorchester `TZO`

family: [swing](#fam-swing) · era: 1925–1955 · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 100–200

German dance orchestras of the 1920s-40s — the German equivalent of swing big bands, with a Central European sensibility and German-language vocals.

**Distinguishing trait:** more 'straight' and orderly than American swing (German precision), and notably played under Nazi-era pressure where jazz was banned as 'degenerate music'.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Two-Beat](rhythmic_feel.md#two-beat) · [Waltz](rhythmic_feel.md#waltz)

### 🤠 Western Swing `WSW`

## Blues

### 😢 Blues `BLU`

family: [blues](#fam-blues) · era: 1900– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 50–130

The foundational African-American form born in the US South from work songs and spirituals, marked by the 12-bar structure, blue notes, and lyrics about real life.

**Distinguishing trait:** cyclic repetition of 12-bar choruses with AAB lyric phrasing and call-and-response between voice and instrument, rather than a dramatic arc.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Blues Shuffle](rhythmic_feel.md#blues-shuffle) · [Slow Drag](rhythmic_feel.md#slow-drag) · [Compound Triplet](rhythmic_feel.md#compound-triplet)

### 🔥 Boogie Woogie `BW`

family: [blues](#fam-blues) · era: 1925– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 100–200

A driving piano style with a repeating 'eight-to-the-bar' ostinato bass in the left hand and improvised blues riffs in the right, built on the 12-bar form.

**Distinguishing trait:** the left hand flows continuously like a motor (vs. stride's leaping bass-chord 'oom-pah'), creating a hypnotic, danceable groove.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Boogie Shuffle](rhythmic_feel.md#boogie-shuffle)

family: [blues](#fam-blues) · era: 1920–1935 · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 60–130

Stage blues from 1920s African-American vaudeville shows — powerhouse female vocalists (Bessie Smith, Ma Rainey) backed by a jazz band.

## Tango (Argentine)

<a id="genre-milonga"></a>

<a id="milonga"></a>

### 🪗 Milonga `MLG`

family: [regional](#fam-regional) · era: 1930– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 110–138

The fast, playful member of the Rioplatense tango family, built on the syncopated habanera bass cell in a brisk 2/4 — earthier and more rhythmic than tango, with a teasing, ground-hugging bounce (Sebastián Piana, Francisco Canaro, Aníbal Troilo). Its double-time subdivision invites the traspié contratiempo double-steps that define the dance, and it shares the Golden-Age orquesta sound and quarter-note tempo range with its tango sibling. Note: "milonga" also names the tango social event itself; here it denotes the music/dance genre.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Tango** | it rides the habanera double-time 2/4 cell with a light, bouncing swing rather than the grounded marcato 4/4 walking pulse. |

<sub>Aliases: Milonga Porteña · Milonga Ciudadana · Milonga Argentina</sub>

<a id="genre-tango"></a>

<a id="tango"></a>

### 🌹 Tango `TGO`

family: [regional](#fam-regional) · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high)

Argentine tango — passionate Rioplatense dance music marked by the bandoneon, dramatic rubato and sharp staccato accents (Carlos Gardel, Astor Piazzolla).

**Distinguishing trait:** built for the tango embrace with its characteristic marcato pulse and bandoneon lead, a specific dance form within the broader South America regional marker.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Tango Marcato](rhythmic_feel.md#tango-marcato) · [Habanera](rhythmic_feel.md#habanera)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [AABA](song_form.md#aaba) · [AABB](song_form.md#aabb)

<a id="genre-vals"></a>

<a id="vals"></a>

### 🎻 Vals `VLS`

family: [regional](#fam-regional) · era: 1900– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 150–240

The Rioplatense tango waltz — a 3/4 tango-family form (vals criollo) played by the same Golden-Age orquestas as tango and milonga, with a continuously flowing, circular lilt felt one-per-bar over a strong beat 1. It is one of the three core tanda types spun at every milonga, ranging from lyrical Fresedo/De Angelis valses to fast, driving D'Arienzo/Biagi readings. Dancers cross-step and turn on the accented beats, gliding around the bar rather than marking each beat.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Tango** | it is in 3/4 felt one-per-bar with a rotary, sweeping flow, where tango is a marcato 4/4 with sharp staccato accents and walked steps. |

<sub>Aliases: Tango Vals · Tango Waltz · Vals Criollo · Vals Cruzado</sub>

## Street Latin

<a id="genre-bachata"></a>

<a id="bachata"></a>

### 🎸 Bachata `BAC`

family: [latin](#fam-latin) · era: 1960– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 108–152

Dominican guitar-based dance music descended from bolero and son, defined by a lead requinto guitar playing high, arpeggiated melodic licks over rhythm guitar, bass, bongó and the scraping güira; long stigmatized as "música de amargue" before Juan Luis Guerra and later Aventura/Romeo Santos carried it worldwide. Its four-beat cycle ends on a syncopated hip-accent ("pop") on the fourth count that the partner dance mirrors with a tap. A core street-latin social-floor genre, often shared on salsa social floors and ranging from traditional acoustic to modern sensual and urban bachata.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Salsa** | requinto-guitar lead and a closed four-count tap-on-four step, where Salsa is horn-and-piano (montuno) driven and locked to the eight-count clave. |

<sub>Aliases: Bachata Dominicana</sub>

<a id="bolero"></a>

### ❤️ Bolero `BOL`

family: [latin](#fam-latin) · era: 1885– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 80–130

A slow, romantic Latin-American ballad born in late-19th-century Santiago de Cuba and spread across the Hispanic world by the guitar trios romanticos and orchestral crooners. Set in 4/4 over a syncopated cinquillo-derived pulse, it foregrounds a lush, sentimental melody and lyrics of love and longing, sung over guitars or a string-and-rhythm orchestra at a languid tempo. As a slow Cuban partner-dance music it underlies the bolero social dance and the codified International and American ballroom Rumba.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Son Cubano** | Bolero is the slow, legato, melody-and-lyric romantic ballad, where Son Cubano is up-tempo dance music driven by the son clave, an anticipated tumbao bass and a call-and-response montuno. |

<sub>Aliases: Bolero Cubano · Cuban Bolero · Bolero Romántico</sub>

<a id="genre-zouk"></a>

<a id="brazilian-zouk"></a>

### 💞 Brazilian Zouk `ZOU`

family: [world](#fam-world) · era: 1980– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 100–130

The music of the Brazilian Zouk floor: a slow, syncopated 4/4 in the zouk lineage, descended from the Antillean zouk of Kassav' and Cape-Verdean coladeira and reshaped through Brazilian lambada into the body-wave "tum-tum-tchi" groove dancers ride today. Its half-time backbeat and floating, melodic phrasing invite long flowing body movement, head rolls and elastic lead-follow connection rather than quick footwork, and modern floors stretch it across zouk-pop and slowed-down R&B and pop remixes at the same tempo.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Kizomba** | it is lambada-derived with a syncopated, count-and-a body-wave groove and floating melodic phrasing, where Kizomba is Angolan semba-derived with a grounded, steady African pulse. |

<sub>Aliases: Zouk · Lambada-Zouk · Zouk-Lambada</sub>

<a id="genre-cha-cha-cha"></a>

<a id="cha-cha-ch"></a>

### 💃 Cha-Cha-Chá `CHA`

family: [latin](#fam-latin) · era: 1953– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 100–135

A Cuban dance-music genre created by violinist Enrique Jorrín around 1953 as a slower, squarer offshoot of danzón-mambo, played by charanga ensembles (flute, strings, piano, güiro, timbales). Its onomatopoeic name comes from the shuffled triple-step the dancers land on the 4-and-1, marked by the güiro and a strong on-beat pulse. It anchors social Cuban/casino floors and is also a codified International Latin ballroom dance.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Mambo** | it is slower, simpler and more on-the-beat with a charanga flute-and-strings sound rather than the faster brass-driven big-band attack, and its signature triple-step replaces mambo's break-step. |

<sub>Aliases: Cha-Cha · Cha Cha · Chachachá · ChaChaCha</sub>

<a id="genre-kizomba"></a>

<a id="kizomba"></a>

### 🫂 Kizomba `KIZ`

family: [world](#fam-world) · era: 1984– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 80–110

An Angolan popular genre that emerged in the early 1980s, blending the local semba tradition with the smooth electronic production and rhythm of French-Caribbean zouk, sung mostly in Portuguese. It rides a slow, grounded 4/4 around 80-110 BPM with a soft, romantic groove built for the close-embrace partner dance of the same name. Its steady, unhurried pulse and warm synth-and-bass texture make it a central pillar of the street-latin social floor.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Zouk** | it keeps a slower, more grounded steady African pulse rooted in semba, where Brazilian zouk runs faster with a syncopated lambada-derived sway. |

<a id="genre-mambo"></a>

<a id="mambo"></a>

### 🎺 Mambo `MAM`

family: [latin](#fam-latin) · era: 1940– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 160–200

Fast, brass-driven Cuban big-band dance music that grew out of the danzón-mambo of the late 1930s and exploded into the orchestral mambo craze of the late 1940s-50s (Pérez Prado, and the New York Afro-Cuban bands of Machito and Tito Puente). It locks a horn-section riff and montuno piano vamp over the clave and a tumbao bass, with punchy syncopated brass accents and call-and-response between sections driving the floor. As the dance that established the "on 2" break-step timing, it is the direct forerunner of New York salsa and a foundational genre of the mambo/salsa floor.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Salsa** | it is the older big-band ancestor, brassier and more orchestral with longer instrumental riff sections, where Salsa is its tighter, song-driven 1970s codification. |

<sub>Aliases: Mambo Cubano · Big-Band Mambo</sub>

<a id="genre-merengue"></a>

<a id="merengue"></a>

### 🥁 Merengue `MER`

family: [latin](#fam-latin) · era: 1850– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 120–170

The national dance music of the Dominican Republic — a fast, driving 2/4 march built on the tambora drum and the scraping güira, originally accordion-led (perico ripiao) and later expanded into horn-driven orquesta and big-band merengue. It carries an even, relentless two-step pulse with marcado bass and saxophone jaleo riffs, made for the simplest and most accessible of the Latin social dances. A floor staple across street-latin nights, played in sets alongside salsa and bachata.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Bachata** | it is a fast 2/4 march on tambora and güira rather than a slower 4/4 ballad led by the requinto guitar. |

<sub>Aliases: Merengue Típico · Perico Ripiao · Merengue Dominicano</sub>

<a id="genre-salsa"></a>

<a id="salsa"></a>

### 💃 Salsa `SAL`

family: [regional](#fam-regional) · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high)

Energetic Afro-Cuban dance music built on the clave, montuno piano and a full horn section, codified in 1970s New York.

**Distinguishing trait:** a clave-locked, horn-driven dance form within the Latin family, faster and brighter than its son and mambo roots.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Latin Clave](rhythmic_feel.md#latin-clave)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Irregular](song_form.md#irregular)

<a id="genre-samba"></a>

<a id="samba-music"></a>

### 🥁 Samba (Music) `SMB`

family: [latin](#fam-latin) · era: 1916– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 96–120

Brazil's signature Afro-Brazilian dance music, born in early-20th-century Rio de Janeiro and inseparable from Carnival, built on a fast 2/4 pulse driven by a surdo-and-tamborim percussion battery with a strong "two" and busy sixteenth-note syncopation. The buoyant, bouncing groove carries social samba no pé and partnered samba de gafieira on the street-latin floor and underpins the codified International Latin ballroom samba. Vocal and pandeiro-led variants (pagode, partido alto) and the percussion-only batucada all share its swinging, springy momentum.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Bossa Nova** | it is a fast, full-throttle percussion-driven dance groove rather than bossa's hushed, slow whispered-guitar comp drawn from the same samba root. |

<sub>Aliases: Samba no Pé · Brazilian Samba</sub>

<a id="genre-son-cubano"></a>

<a id="son-cubano"></a>

### 🪘 Son Cubano `SON`

family: [latin](#fam-latin) · era: 1900– · dance: [high](#ax-dance_relevance-high) · bpm: 140–180

The foundational Afro-Cuban genre from eastern Cuba's Oriente, built on the son clave, the tres guitar's syncopated guajeo, and a two-part form that opens with a sung verse and breaks into an open call-and-response montuno over anticipated tumbao bass and bongó/clave percussion (Trío Matamoros, Sexteto Habanero, Arsenio Rodríguez, later Buena Vista Social Club). It is the trunk from which mambo, cha-cha-chá, and salsa all grew, and it remains a living social-floor music in the casino/salsa world, danced grounded on the contratiempo.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Salsa** | son is the slower, sparser, tres-led acoustic root, where salsa is its faster, brighter, horn-section-driven New York codification of the same clave-and-montuno lineage. |

<sub>Aliases: Cuban Son · Son Montuno</sub>

<a id="genre-latin"></a>

<a id="latin"></a>

### 🪘 Latin `LAT`

family: [regional](#fam-regional) · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium)

A broad marker for Latin American popular and dance music — salsa, mambo, cha-cha, merengue and related Afro-Caribbean styles.

**Distinguishing trait:** a wide regional/stylistic umbrella usually paired with a more specific genre, broader than its sub-forms like Salsa or Latin Jazz.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Latin Clave](rhythmic_feel.md#latin-clave) · [Bossa Nova](rhythmic_feel.md#bossa-nova)

<a id="genre-latin-jazz"></a>

<a id="latin-jazz"></a>

### 🪘 Latin Jazz `LtJ`

family: [latin](#fam-latin) · era: 1947– · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 100–220

The fusion of Afro-Cuban rhythms (clave, son, rumba) with jazz harmony and improvisation (Dizzy Gillespie with Chano Pozo, Machito).

**Distinguishing trait:** everything is locked to the asymmetric clave pattern over a montuno piano vamp and tumbao bass, climaxing in a mambo/coro call-and-response (vs. swing's 4-beat walking bass).

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Latin Clave](rhythmic_feel.md#latin-clave) · [Bossa Nova](rhythmic_feel.md#bossa-nova)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [AABA](song_form.md#aaba) · [12-bar Blues](song_form.md#12-bar-blues)

<sub>Aliases: Afro-Cuban Jazz · Cubop</sub>

<a id="genre-rumba-cubana"></a>

<a id="rumba-cuban"></a>

### 🪘 Rumba (Cuban) `RMB`

family: [latin](#fam-latin) · era: 1880– · dance: [medium](#ax-dance_relevance-medium) · bpm: 90–130

Secular Afro-Cuban folkloric music from the dockside solares of Havana and Matanzas, built entirely on layered congas/cajón, claves, palitos and call-and-response vocals with no melodic instruments. Its three sub-forms drive distinct dances: the flirtatious couple guaguancó, the slow elder yambú, and the acrobatic solo male columbia. Danced socially in rumba gatherings and as the percussive root feeding casino/Afro-Cuban movement.

**Distinguishing trait**

| vs | how it differs |
|---|---|
| **Son** | the rumba is pure percussion and voice over a 2-bar rumba clave with no melodic instruments, where the son adds tres/guitar, bass and a sung melody. |

<sub>Aliases: Rumba Cubana · Cuban Rumba · Guaguancó · Yambú · Columbia · Rumba Guaguancó</sub>

### 🎷 Hard Bop `HB`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 1953–1965 · dance: [low](#ax-dance_relevance-low) · bpm: 120–240

A late-1950s East Coast reaction to 'too cool' jazz — a return to blues and gospel roots while keeping bebop's complexity (Art Blakey's Jazz Messengers).

**Distinguishing trait:** more emotional, groovy and funky than pure bebop ('bebop with soul'), with more blues forms, gospel harmonies and catchier themes.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Hard Bop Swing](rhythmic_feel.md#hard-bop-swing) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Walking 4](rhythmic_feel.md#walking-4)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [12-bar Blues](song_form.md#12-bar-blues) · [AABA](song_form.md#aaba) · [Rhythm Changes](song_form.md#rhythm-changes)

<a id="jazz-fusion"></a>

### ⚗️ Jazz Fusion `FUS`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 1968– · dance: [low](#ax-dance_relevance-low) · bpm: 80–160

The 1970s merging of jazz with rock, funk and electronics (Miles Davis 'Bitches Brew'), the most commercially successful jazz since the swing era.

**Distinguishing trait:** electrification (Fender Rhodes, synths, electric guitar/bass) with straight-eighth rock/funk rhythms and longer, groove-based rather than chord-based improvisations.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Straight 4](rhythmic_feel.md#straight-4) · [Backbeat](rhythmic_feel.md#backbeat)

**Typical song forms:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Irregular](song_form.md#irregular)

### 🌀 Modal Jazz `MOD`

family: [jazz](#fam-jazz) · era: 1958– · dance: [low](#ax-dance_relevance-low) · bpm: 80–180

A 1960s approach improvising over scales (modes) instead of complex chord progressions (Miles Davis 'Kind of Blue', the best-selling jazz album ever).

**Distinguishing trait:** long stretches on a single chord/mode give a spacious, meditative, 'floating' feel and more rhythmic freedom, with horizontal scalar melodic lines.

**Typical feels:** [⚠](#expert-review-pending "Expert review pending") [Straight 4](rhythmic_feel.md#straight-4) · [Jazz Swing 8ths](rhythmic_feel.md#jazz-swing-8ths) · [Walking 4](rhythmic_feel.md#walking-4)

## Genre families

What each `family` chip groups.

- <a id="fam-blues"></a>**Blues** — The foundational African-American form built on the 12-bar cycle, blue notes and call-and-response, from acoustic Delta and Chicago electric to boogie-woogie and jump.
- <a id="fam-classical"></a>**Classical** — Western art music in the orchestral and chamber tradition — through-composed, fully-notated works for orchestra or ensemble, from Baroque through Romantic and beyond.
- <a id="fam-country"></a>**Country** — American roots music with European balladry and African-American influence, marked by twangy/nasal vocals, fiddle and steel guitar over storytelling lyrics.
- <a id="fam-dance"></a>**Dance** — Floor-function music made for sustained dancing, mostly electronic-leaning and built on a four-on-the-floor pulse — house, disco and the broader dance umbrella.
- <a id="fam-electro"></a>**Electro** — Electronically produced, synth-driven music with programmed beats, including the swing- and jazz-sampling electro-swing subset.
- <a id="fam-electronic"></a>**Electronic** — DAW-produced, synth-driven music built from programmed beats and samples rather than live performance, spanning house, techno and related styles.
- <a id="fam-folk"></a>**Folk** — Acoustic, vocal, narrative-driven music rooted in national traditions and the mid-century revival, typically guitar-led and song-centred.
- <a id="fam-gospel"></a>**Gospel** — Sacred African-American church music marked by powerful choirs, melismatic shout vocals and call-and-response over a building vamp.
- <a id="fam-jazz"></a>**Jazz** — Improvised African-American music built on swing feel and soloing, from New Orleans collective playing through bebop, cool, hard bop and beyond.
- <a id="fam-latin"></a>**Latin** — Latin American and Afro-Caribbean dance music locked to clave-based rhythms — salsa, mambo, cha-cha, merengue and related styles.
- <a id="fam-misc"></a>**Misc** — A catch-all for music that breaks with conventional form or doesn't fit any other family, including experimental and avant-garde work.
- <a id="fam-novelty"></a>**Novelty** — Comedy, gimmick and one-off songs whose appeal is the joke, sound effect or stunt rather than a musical style.
- <a id="fam-pop"></a>**Pop** — Vocal-led, hook-driven mainstream popular music in verse-chorus form, plus the orchestral easy-listening and standards canon aimed at broad appeal.
- <a id="fam-r-and-b"></a>**R And B** — Rhythm-and-blues-derived groove music centred on a heavy, syncopated backbeat over melody, including funk and related dance-floor styles.
- <a id="fam-ragtime"></a>**Ragtime** — Composed, fully-notated piano music with a syncopated right-hand melody over a steady march-like oom-pah bass — the pre-jazz parlour style.
- <a id="fam-regional"></a>**Regional** — Origin-based context markers (such as Slovenian, ex-Yugoslav, South American or Argentine tango) that flag where music comes from and usually accompany a genre tag.
- <a id="fam-rnb"></a>**Rnb** — Post-1947 African-American popular music joining blues, jazz and gospel with a strong backbeat and electric instruments — the bridge from jump blues toward soul and rock.
- <a id="fam-rock"></a>**Rock** — Guitar-driven music in verse-chorus form built on a strong backbeat, from rockabilly and early rock'n'roll through classic, punk and alternative strains.
- <a id="fam-soul"></a>**Soul** — Gospel-rooted secular vocal music with expressive, melismatic singing over a tight rhythm section and horn arrangements.
- <a id="fam-swing"></a>**Swing** — Big-band and combo jazz with a four-beat lilting groove and section-based arrangements, plus its later revivals built for partner dancing.
- <a id="fam-urban"></a>**Urban** — Beat-and-sample-based music built around rhythmic spoken vocals, covering hip-hop and rap.
- <a id="fam-world"></a>**World** — Traditional and popular music from non-Western traditions outside the other families, chiefly the Jamaican lineage of reggae and ska.
